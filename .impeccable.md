## Design Context

### Users
Casual puzzle game players on desktop (Linux, Windows, macOS). They're looking for a satisfying, meditative puzzle experience — something to unwind with. Age range skews 20-45. They appreciate clean interfaces and don't want to be overwhelmed. They may play in short sessions or marathon through chapters.

### Brand Personality
**Minimal. Mysterious. Meditative.**

The game presents itself as a clean puzzle app — but hides a deeper story about repairing a spaceship carrying sleeping humans. The twist is revealed gradually in the final chapter. Until then, everything should feel like a polished, modern puzzle game with no sci-fi hints.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Visual tone**: Clean, dark, calm. Think Mini Metro meets Monument Valley. Not flashy — confident.
- **Theme**: Dark mode only. The 3D board with colored tiles and moving bots IS the visual identity.
- **References**: Mini Metro (minimal transport puzzler), Monument Valley (elegant puzzle game), Baba Is You (charming puzzle mechanics)
- **Anti-references**: Generic mobile puzzle games with ads. Sci-fi cockpit UIs. Anything that screams "AI made this" (dark mode + teal glow + glassmorphism)
- **Font**: Fira Sans (embedded, single weight). Clean, readable, no-nonsense.
- **Accent color**: Teal (0.18, 0.54, 0.48) — the game's signature. Used sparingly for primary actions.
- **Framework**: Bevy 0.18 built-in UI (flexbox-like nodes, not HTML/CSS). No gradients, no blur, no glassmorphism — the engine doesn't support them natively.

### Design Principles
1. **The board is the hero** — UI exists to support the 3D puzzle board, never to compete with it. Keep UI minimal and transparent.
2. **Quiet confidence** — No flashy animations or attention-grabbing effects. Movement should be slow, deliberate, calming. The game doesn't need to sell itself — it draws you in.
3. **Consistency over decoration** — Every button, label, and panel should use the centralized theme (ui_theme.rs). No one-off colors or sizes.
4. **Preserve the mystery** — Nothing in the UI, copy, or visual design should hint at the spaceship/repair story. It's a puzzle game. Period. Until it isn't.
5. **Respect the player** — Clean typography, clear hierarchy, obvious affordances. Don't make them guess what's clickable. Don't waste their time.
